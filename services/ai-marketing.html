WHY AI MATTERS NOW

AI Is Moving From Experimentation Into Everyday Business

The shift toward AI is not theoretical. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI research found that 88% of respondents said their organizations were regularly using AI in at least one business function, up from 78% in 2024. The same research found that 79% reported regular generative AI use in at least one business function.

Marketing and sales are especially important in that transition. Across industries surveyed by McKinsey, marketing and sales was the most commonly reported business function for generative AI use. In 2024, 55% of respondents reported regular generative AI use in marketing and sales.

But adoption does not automatically create value. McKinsey's latest research also shows that most organizations are still experimenting or piloting AI, while only a small share report that AI has been fully scaled across the organization. That creates an important distinction: using AI is not the same thing as using AI well.

For businesses, the opportunity is to move beyond collecting AI tools and toward designing useful workflows around real business objectives.

INDUSTRY TREND

AI Adoption Is Accelerating

Reported organizational AI use has risen sharply in McKinsey's global surveys, while generative AI has moved rapidly into business functions such as marketing and sales.

The strategic question is no longer simply whether AI is being adopted. It is where AI should be applied, how it should be governed, and how people should work with it.

THE AI ADOPTION TREND

The Market Is Moving. The Smart Response Is Strategic.

McKinsey's Global Survey on AI shows how quickly reported AI use has expanded. The trend is important, but the numbers should not be interpreted as a reason to automate everything. They show that AI is becoming part of the normal business environment—and that businesses need a thoughtful approach to deciding where it belongs.

Source: McKinsey Global Survey on AI, 2023–2025. Percentages represent respondents reporting organizational use in at least one business function; survey definitions and samples vary by year.

The takeaway is not that every business needs more AI. The takeaway is that AI is becoming a standard part of the competitive environment. Businesses that learn where it creates value—and where human judgment remains essential—will be better positioned to use it intentionally.

HUMAN INTELLIGENCE + ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

AI Should Strengthen Human Decision-Making, Not Replace It

The most useful role for AI in marketing is not to remove the human from the process. It is to help people process more information, explore more possibilities, identify patterns, reduce repetitive work, and make decisions with better context.

Marketing still requires judgment. People understand the nuances of a brand, the realities of a market, the meaning behind customer behavior, and the consequences of a decision in ways an automated system cannot fully replicate.

AI can help surface information. Humans decide what that information means, whether it is trustworthy, how it fits the business, and what should happen next.

AI-assisted research and analysis
Data-informed marketing decisions
Human oversight where judgment matters

RESPONSIBLE AI

Automation Does Not Eliminate Judgment

AI systems can produce useful results, but they can also produce incorrect, incomplete, outdated, or inappropriate information. Responsible implementation requires understanding those limitations.

Our approach keeps human oversight where accuracy, judgment, brand integrity, customer relationships, privacy, or business consequences require it.

The goal is not to automate everything. The goal is to determine what should be automated, what should remain human-led, and how the two can work together effectively.

OUR AI IMPLEMENTATION PROCESS

From Business Problem to Working AI Solution

We approach AI implementation as a business and workflow exercise, not simply a technology purchase. The process is designed to make sure the technology has a purpose before it becomes part of the business.

Phase 01 — Identify

Determine where the business is experiencing inefficiency, friction, missed opportunities, or information bottlenecks.

Phase 02 — Evaluate

Determine whether AI is actually appropriate for the problem and identify potential tools, integrations, and approaches.

Phase 03 — Design

Define the workflow, inputs, outputs, human oversight, system interactions, data requirements, and success criteria.

Phase 04 — Implement

Integrate the selected solution into the appropriate marketing, website, analytics, or business workflow.

Phase 05 — Test

Evaluate system behavior, accuracy, workflow performance, usability, security considerations, and failure conditions.

Phase 06 — Optimize

Monitor results and refine the system as business requirements, technology, and available data evolve.
