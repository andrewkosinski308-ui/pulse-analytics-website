THE STARTUP CHALLENGE

Startups Cannot Afford to Market Without a Strategy

Early-stage businesses typically operate with limited resources, incomplete information, and constantly changing priorities.

Every dollar, hour, campaign, and technology decision needs to contribute toward learning, validating demand, acquiring customers, or building the infrastructure necessary for future growth.

The goal is not to do everything at once. The goal is to build the right things in the right order.

STARTUP VIABILITY — THE DATA

Survival Is Not Guaranteed. Strategy Can Improve What You Control.

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data show why the early years matter. In its Business Employment Dynamics analysis, the five-year survival rate for the 2018 startup cohort was 57.3%. More recent BLS cohort data, reported from the same establishment survival series, show approximately 51.4% of establishments from the March 2020 cohort were still operating five years later. Survival is not the same thing as profitability or startup success, but the data makes one point unmistakable: the early operating period is a high-risk environment.

The strategic implication: Pulse Analytics cannot guarantee that a startup will survive. What we can do is help founders reduce avoidable uncertainty: research the market before committing heavily, establish measurable customer-acquisition systems, track evidence, identify weak assumptions, and use real market behavior to improve the strategy.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Business Employment Dynamics; U.S. Census Bureau — Business Dynamics Statistics; CB Insights — The Top 9 Reasons Startups Fail, 2026.

WHAT THE FAILURE DATA REVEALS

The Biggest Risks Are Often Connected.

CB Insights' March 2026 analysis of 431 VC-backed startups that shut down since 2023 found that running out of capital was the most common reported factor, at 70%. But capital depletion was often an end result rather than the underlying problem. Poor product-market fit appeared in 43% of the failures, bad timing in 29%, and unsustainable unit economics in 19%. Because companies could cite multiple reasons, the percentages are not intended to sum to 100%.

Ran out of capital — 70%

Runway can disappear when revenue, economics, or market assumptions do not develop as planned.

Poor product-market fit — 43%

Weak market fit can prevent early traction from becoming durable demand.

Bad timing — 29%

Even strong ideas can struggle when market conditions or customer readiness are misaligned.

Unsustainable unit economics — 19%

Growth becomes dangerous when the economics of acquiring and serving customers do not support the model.

Source: CB Insights, The Top 9 Reasons Startups Fail. The analysis covers 431 VC-backed companies that publicly shut down since 2023; failure factors can overlap.
